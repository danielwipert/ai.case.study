## 1. Summary of the record

> Scope note, not a finding: this summary retells a few rows that the headings below retell in full; it adds nothing to them. Each sentence carries the codes of the rows it rests on.

> Unless marked otherwise, statements below come from D01, the LungIMPACT investigators' peer-reviewed trial report in Nature Medicine.

Between 17 July 2023 and 31 December 2024, 97,731 chest X-rays were performed
across five NHS trusts in the LungIMPACT trial `[F001]`. Participants were aged
18 years or older and were attending for a primary care-requested chest X-ray
`[F071]`. The investigators reported that participants were block-randomized
by day and site to either immediate AI prioritization of reporting or no AI
prioritization `[F074]`, and elsewhere that individual patients were not
randomized, and that their chest X-rays were; each statement contradicts the
other `[F074]` `[F077]`. One AI product was used in the trial: qXR, from
Qure.ai Technologies `[F031]`.

There was no significant difference in the time from chest X-ray to CT scan
according to AI prioritization, with a ratio of geometric means of 0.97, 95
percent confidence interval 0.93 to 1.02, P = 0.31 `[F013]`. There was no
significant difference in the time from chest X-ray to lung cancer diagnosis
between AI-prioritization days and days without AI prioritization, with a
ratio of geometric means of 0.98, 95 percent confidence interval 0.83 to 1.16,
P = 0.84 `[F016]`. Table 2 of D01 gives the median time from chest X-ray to
chest X-ray report as 34.1 hours on AI-prioritization days and 47.0 hours on
days without AI prioritization, with P < 0.001 `[F023]`.

The investigators reported that no statistically significant differences were
found for either primary outcome in any quarter, which Extended Data Table 2
of D01 contradicts for time to CT scan in the third quarter of 2024 `[F017]`
`[F019]`. The investigators reported that there were no differences in
secondary outcomes by AI group, which Table 2 of D01 contradicts for the time
from chest X-ray to chest X-ray report `[F022]` `[F023]`.

The investigators state that "AI prioritization of CXR requested by UK primary
care has no significant impact on the lung cancer pathway" `[F124]`. The
investigators state that "It has been shown conclusively that AI
prioritization had no impact on time to CT or time to lung cancer diagnosis"
`[F130]`. The investigators state that a health economics evaluation of the
trial will be published separately `[F160]`.

> Unless marked otherwise, statements below come from D01, the LungIMPACT investigators' peer-reviewed trial report in Nature Medicine.

The LungIMPACT trial was a prospective, multicenter, randomized controlled
trial conducted between July 2023 and December 2024 across five NHS Trusts in
England `[F070]`. Between 17 July 2023 and 31 December 2024, 97,731 chest
X-rays were performed across five NHS trusts `[F001]`. Patients were followed
up until 5 June 2025 `[F002]`. Participants were aged 18 years or older and
were attending for a primary care-requested chest X-ray `[F071]`.
Anteroposterior or posteroanterior chest X-ray views were analyzed, and lateral
views were excluded `[F072]`. After data cleaning, 93,326 chest X-rays from
86,945 patients were included in the analysis `[F003]`. The mean age of the
study population was 59 years, and 46 percent of the study population were
male `[F005]` `[F012]`. The study chest X-rays were performed 54 percent at
University Hospitals of Leicester, 15 percent at Nottingham University
Hospitals, 11 percent at East Sussex and North Essex Foundation Trust, 11
percent at University College London Hospital and 9 percent at University
Hospitals of Birmingham `[F006]`. No data on race, ethnicity or social grouping
were collected in the trial `[F073]`.

The investigators reported that participants aged 18 years or older, attending
for a primary care-requested chest X-ray, were block-randomized by day and site
to either immediate AI prioritization of reporting or no AI prioritization
`[F074]`. The investigators also reported that individual patients were not
randomized, and that their chest X-rays were, which contradicts their report
that participants were block-randomized; D01 states both, and the two
statements disagree on whether participants or their chest X-rays were
randomized `[F077]` `[F074]`. Allocation of Monday-to-Friday routine imaging
whole-day sessions to AI prioritization or routine care was made in advance, by
random sampling with a 1:1 randomization method `[F075]`. The investigators
state that "Days were randomly allocated to minimize the potential impact of
different levels of staffing and capacity." `[F076]` On the library's reading
of D01, as of 30 September 2026, D01 reports neither the randomization block
size nor the number of days or sessions allocated to each arm `[F117]`. Of the
93,326 analysed chest X-rays, 45,987, 49.3 percent, were performed in sessions
with AI prioritization for immediate review `[F004]`. On the library's
computation, 47,339 of the analysed chest X-rays were performed in sessions
without AI prioritization, computed as the 93,326 analysed chest X-rays less
the 45,987 in AI-prioritization sessions `[F011]`. Blinding was not possible,
the intervention being radiology worklist prioritisation, and radiology
clinicians were blinded to the randomisation schedule/sequence `[F080]`. Dates
of CT scans and of lung cancer diagnosis were verified by the investigators
manually and without knowledge of the study arm `[F081]`. Consent was not
obtained from patients; in each radiology department, messaging stated that AI
was being used as part of a research study and gave details of how to opt out,
as agreed by the Ethics Committee `[F078]`.

The investigators reported that the AI was applied in both arms at the time of
image acquisition, so that the reporter had access to the AI-marked images at
the time of reporting in both arms `[F083]`. Immediate review of the chest X-ray was permitted, according to
local procedure, in both arms of the trial `[F084]`. Extended Data Fig. 2 of
D01 sets out a pathway in which, under "AI Prioritisation flag", the chest
X-ray is checked by the radiographer with the qXR AI read, and under "AI at
time of reporting session", it is checked by the radiographer without the qXR
AI read and later reported with the qXR AI read `[F085]`. All imaging, chest
X-ray or CT, was performed as part of routine care, with the final decision
made by the clinicians `[F086]`.

The investigators reported that the primary outcomes were the difference in
time, in days from chest X-ray acquisition, to lung cancer diagnosis and to CT,
between chest X-rays with AI support at acquisition and prioritization for
immediate review and those with no immediate read and with the AI read
available at the time of reporting `[F087]`. The investigators reported, in the
Reporting Summary, that the primary outcomes were the difference in time, in
days from chest X-ray request, to the diagnosis of lung cancer and to CT,
which contradicts the time origin of chest X-ray acquisition given in the
other statement; D01 states both, and the two disagree on the point from which
the primary outcomes are timed `[F088]` `[F087]`. The secondary outcomes were:
days from chest X-ray acquisition to urgent lung cancer (2WW) referral; days
from chest X-ray acquisition to cancer treatment start; agreement between qXR
and human readers for present/absent findings, by classification; number of
urgent lung cancer (2WW) referrals; incidence of lung cancer; stage of lung
cancer at diagnosis; and algorithm sensitivity, specificity and agreement
between report and AI (Kappa) `[F089]`. On the library's reading of D01, as of
30 September 2026, D01's Methods and its Reporting Summary do not list time
from chest X-ray to chest X-ray report among the trial's outcomes, although
Table 2 of D01 reports that time under "Secondary outcomes" `[F090]`.

The time outcomes were log-transformed and compared with a two-sided t test of
the null hypothesis that means on the log scale are equal, presented as the
ratio of geometric means with 95 percent confidence intervals; the
investigators gave right skew as the reason for the log transformation
`[F091]`. The secondary time endpoints, time to urgent referral and time to
treatment starting, were assessed with a t test on log-transformed time data
`[F098]`. The number of urgent referrals, the incidence of lung cancer and the
stage of lung cancer at diagnosis were compared between the two groups using
chi-squared tests `[F099]`. Agreement between the reporter and the AI was
summarized as overall agreement, measured with the kappa statistic `[F100]`.
No statistical testing was carried out on the baseline characteristics, which
were tabulated overall and by intervention group in Table 1 `[F105]`. The
investigators reported that the statistical analysis was conducted by an
independent statistician (LS) without input from the study investigators
`[F082]`.

For the main analysis of the primary outcomes, time to CT and time to cancer
diagnosis were calculated from each patient's first chest X-ray, with the
investigators' stated aim of avoiding duplication and overcounting of outcomes
`[F092]`. Sensitivity analyses on multiple chest X-rays per patient were

The primary outcome time to CT scan was further examined by restricting the

flagged for CT referral `[F094]`. The investigators reported that on manual
review of the data, when the time to CT exceeded 14 days, the CT was never
triggered by the index chest X-ray randomized for the trial `[F095]`. The
investigators state, in the Discussion, that "We therefore had to complete
restrictive sensitivity analyses using the code, where used, signifying a CT

`[F096]` On the library's reading of D01, as of 30 September 2026, D01 does
not state whether these two restricted analyses of time to CT were

The investigators reported that the sample size for time to lung cancer

The investigators reported, in Figure 1 of D01 (the CONSORT diagram), that

## 8. Outcomes and measured effects

> Scope note, not a finding: this section retells rows F007 to F010, F013 to F017 and F019 to F025, from claims C07 to C21 of D01, and row F018 from heading 11. > Unless marked otherwise, statements below come from D01, the LungIMPACT investigators' peer-reviewed trial report in Nature Medicine.

The time-to-CT analysis included 13,347 patients who had a valid CT scan,
6,674 counted under AI-prioritization days and 6,673 under days without
`[F007]`. The median time from chest X-ray to CT scan was 53 days,
interquartile range 17 to 145, on AI-prioritization days and 53 days,
interquartile range 19 to 141, on days without AI prioritization `[F009]`.
There was no significant difference in the time from chest X-ray to CT scan
according to AI prioritization, with a ratio of geometric means of 0.97, 95
percent confidence interval 0.93 to 1.02, P = 0.31 `[F013]`. On the library's
reading of D01's text, its Table 2 and its Extended Data Tables 1, 2 and 4, as
of 30 September 2026, D01 does not state which arm of the trial is the
numerator of the ratios of geometric means it reports `[F018]`. When the
analysis was restricted to CT scans performed within 14 days, the
investigators reported no difference in time to CT scan between
AI-prioritization days and days without AI prioritization, P = 0.96 `[F014]`.
When it was restricted to chest X-rays with a CT referral, the investigators
reported no difference in time to CT scan between AI-prioritization days and
days without AI prioritization, P = 0.86 `[F015]`.

The time-to-diagnosis analysis included 558 patients diagnosed with lung
cancer, 269 counted under AI-prioritization days and 289 under days without
`[F008]`. The median time from chest X-ray to lung cancer diagnosis was 44
days, interquartile range 26 to 90, on AI-prioritization days and 46 days,
interquartile range 24 to 105, on days without AI prioritization `[F010]`.
There was no significant difference in the time from chest X-ray to lung
cancer diagnosis between AI-prioritization days and days without AI
prioritization, with a ratio of geometric means of 0.98, 95 percent confidence

## 9. Statements by the parties

> Scope note, not a finding: this section retells rows F124 to F152, all of them Statement rows taken from the abstract and the Discussion of D01. Each is retold as something the investigators state, in their own words, and none is retold as a fact about the trial. The ledger's labels on these rows, not this text, carry any assessment of them.

> Unless marked otherwise, statements below come from D01, the LungIMPACT investigators' peer-reviewed trial report in Nature Medicine.

> Conclusions.

The investigators state, in the abstract of D01, that "AI
prioritization of CXR requested by UK primary care has no significant impact
on the lung cancer pathway." `[F124]` The investigators state, in the
Discussion of D01, that "This large, multisite randomized study has shown that
AI prioritization of CXRs did not improve the speed of the lung cancer
diagnostic pathway." `[F127]` The investigators state, in the Discussion, that
"It has been shown conclusively that AI prioritization had no impact on time
to CT or time to lung cancer diagnosis." `[F130]` The investigators state, in
the Discussion, that "Despite this limitation, the results clearly show no
difference in time from CXR to CT according to prioritization day across all
analyses, each of which included a more than adequate number of observations
(Table 3)." `[F144]` The investigators state, in the Discussion, that "A
significant reduction in the median time from CXR acquisition to report was
observed, from 47 h to 34.1 h; however, no significant differences were
detected in any of the timings measured as primary or secondary outcomes
(Table 2)." `[F131]` The investigators state, in the Discussion, that "The
LungIMPACT trial has shown that immediate AI prioritization had no impact on
important clinical outcomes for patients with suspected lung cancer in the
English NHS and that this tool, as assessed, does not currently require
potentially time-consuming and costly efforts to include in clinical
services." `[F147]`

The investigators state, in the abstract of D01, that "Therefore, CXR AI
deployments should not include worklist prioritization in this context."
`[F125]` The investigators state, in the Discussion, that "This means that
this element of AI functionality, which introduces additional complexity and
cost to AI installation and may add time to clinical workflows, is not
required to accelerate the lung cancer diagnostic pathway." `[F128]` The
investigators state, in the Discussion, that "costs are both considerable and
avoidable, given the results reported in this study". `[F129]`

> Explanations offered.

The investigators state, in the Discussion, that
"The lack of improvement in AI prioritization is likely because, although the
time to CXR report was shortened, it was not sufficiently reduced in the
prioritization arm to influence the clinical pathway in the same way as
immediate radiographer triage did in our previous study". `[F132]` After
proposing that explanation, the investigators state that "This may, in turn,
be explained by the limited capacity within Trusts to both report immediately
and organize downstream tests and appointments, even though this is a
recommendation in the NOLCP." `[F133]` The investigators state, in the
Discussion, that "Because AI prioritization, although associated with a
shortened time to CXR report, did not influence more important clinical
outcomes, services would need to implement additional pathway changes to
potentially replicate our previous findings." `[F150]` The investigators
state, in the Discussion, that "A recommendation could be that any AI-flagged
abnormality prompts an immediate human review and, if confirmed, the
implementation of a downstream bundle of investigations." `[F151]`

> The design and its strengths.

The investigators state, in the Discussion,
that "immediate review of the CXR was permitted, according to local procedure,
in both arms of the study. This ensured that AI prioritization was tested
rather than the effect of primary pathway change." `[F134]` The investigators
state, in the Discussion, that "The automated data acquisition followed by
manual verification is another strength of the study and the independent
analysis of the results with no influence from either the AI developers or the
investigators." `[F136]` The investigators state, in the Discussion, that
"Another major strength is the number of discordance reviews completed by
expert radiologists and reporting radiographers. This has provided perhaps the
largest reliable dataset on this aspect." `[F137]`

> Limitations.

The investigators state, in the Discussion, that "A
limitation of the study is that the primary outcomes evaluated only the impact
of AI prioritization, not the presence or absence of AI in the clinical
workflow. This was necessary because of the study’s nonconsenting nature."
`[F138]` The investigators state, in the Discussion, that "By choosing time to
CT as an additional primary outcome, we can be more confident that a
comparison of AI use versus no AI would not show a meaningful difference."
`[F139]` The investigators state, in the Discussion, that "A limitation is the
use of a single AI product, which will inevitably have different performance
characteristics from others." `[F140]` The investigators state, in the
Discussion, that "However, the fact that only prioritization was tested here
means differences in AI accuracy had less effect on the coprimary outcomes
than on the discordances." `[F141]` The investigators state, in the
Discussion, that "A further limitation of the study was the difficulty in
identifying the best way to measure time to CT." `[F142]` The investigators
state, in the Discussion, that "While this finding is of interest in itself,
it may have diluted this primary outcome by adding many CTs that were not
relevant to the hypothesis." `[F143]` The investigators state, in the
Discussion, that "Another limitation relates to the discordance reviews, which
have been reported according to differences between the AI flag
classification and the final report. Because reporters had the AI available at
the time, we cannot determine how many findings would be independently found
by the AI or whether the human would have found something without AI
assistance." `[F145]` The investigators state, in the Discussion, that "Our
study is unable to determine whether the screening program is reducing the
early-stage detection by CXR." `[F146]`

> Wider claims.

The investigators state, in the Discussion, that "This is
likely to apply to the use of any CXR algorithm in this way, because the vast
majority of cancers were detected where AI was abnormal and prioritization was
therefore tested in a large number of cases." `[F148]` The investigators
state, in the Discussion, that "Furthermore, this finding may extend to other
settings where there is a relatively rapid review of images through the usual
clinical pathway." `[F149]` The investigators state, in the Discussion, that
"However, feedback from reporters indicated that most of the FPs are rapidly
dismissed, so the potential for extra workload will be dependent on the user
becoming familiar with the strengths and weaknesses of the AI." `[F135]` The
investigators state, in the Discussion, that "The large-scale analysis of
secondary outcomes shows the considerable potential burden of FP results,
using radiologist and radiographer review as the gold standard, while also
showing the considerable number of lung cancers diagnosed in this
medium-to-high-risk group." `[F152]` The investigators state, in the abstract
of D01, that "Future research should differentiate between primary pathway
changes and the direct impact of AI." `[F126]`
